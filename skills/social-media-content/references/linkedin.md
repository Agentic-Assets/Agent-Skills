# LinkedIn — Platform Guide

## Format constraints

- **Post character limit**: 3,000 characters (roughly 500 words)
- **First ~210 characters** show before "...see more" — this is your hook
- **Articles**: Long-form (no limit), but posts typically outperform articles for engagement
- **Document posts** (carousel/PDF): Upload a PDF that users swipe through; very high engagement
- **Polls**: Up to 4 options, 1-2 week duration

## What works for academic + business content on LinkedIn

LinkedIn's audience is professional and skews toward people who want to learn something or feel connected to someone's journey. The best-performing content combines genuine expertise with a human angle.

### High-performing formats

1. **Insight post**: Share a specific finding, lesson, or observation with enough context that a non-specialist gets it
2. **Contrarian take**: Challenge a common assumption in your field — "Everyone says X, but the data shows Y"
3. **Behind-the-scenes**: The research process, a consulting challenge you solved (anonymized), what you learned teaching a concept
4. **Milestone + reflection**: Paper accepted, grant funded, new project launched — but focus on the insight, not the brag
5. **Curated knowledge**: "3 papers every real estate investor should read this quarter" with brief summaries

### What underperforms on LinkedIn

- Pure self-promotion without a takeaway
- Vague inspirational quotes
- Posts that start with "I'm humbled to announce..."
- Dense academic jargon without translation
- Anything that reads like a press release

## Post structure

LinkedIn rewards readability. Use this general structure:

**Hook** (first 2 lines): Lead with the most interesting thing. This is what shows before "see more."

**Body** (5-15 lines): The content. Use short paragraphs (1-3 sentences each). White space is your friend — dense blocks of text don't get read on mobile.

**Closer** (1-2 lines): A question, call to action, or concise takeaway.

**Hashtags** (last line): 3-5 relevant hashtags.

## Formatting tips

- Break text into short paragraphs with blank lines between them
- One idea per paragraph
- Use bold sparingly for emphasis (LinkedIn supports basic formatting)
- Numbered lists work if the content is genuinely list-shaped
- Don't use bullet points for things that should be sentences
- Start lines with a capital letter — mid-sentence line breaks look careless

## Hook formulas that work

- **The surprising stat**: "Commercial real estate cap rates just hit their highest level since 2019."
- **The contrarian**: "Most people think AI will replace real estate analysts. I think it'll make them more valuable."
- **The question**: "What's the single best predictor of REIT performance? It's not what you think."
- **The story start**: "Last week, a student asked me a question I couldn't answer."
- **The framework**: "I evaluate every multifamily deal with three questions."

## Hashtag strategy

- 3-5 hashtags, placed at the end of the post
- Mix broad and niche: #RealEstate + #MultifamilyInvesting, #AI + #PropTech
- Good tags for this profile: #RealEstate #Finance #AI #MachineLearning #HigherEd #CRE #PropTech #Sustainability #Econometrics #AgenticAI

## Posting cadence and timing

- Best times: Tuesday through Thursday, 8-10am CT
- 2-3 posts per week is the sweet spot
- Engage with comments within the first hour — LinkedIn's algorithm heavily favors early engagement
- Comment on others' posts regularly; it builds visibility and relationships

## Voice on LinkedIn

LinkedIn is more polished than X but should still feel human. Write like you're explaining something interesting to a smart colleague over coffee — not like you're submitting to a journal or writing ad copy.

**Good**: "I've been studying how political connections affect firm value for the past five years. The most surprising finding isn't that connections help — it's when they don't. Here's what the data shows..."

**Bad**: "This paper investigates the heterogeneous effects of corporate political connections on firm value across varying institutional contexts, employing a difference-in-differences framework..."

Also bad: "🚀 EXCITING NEWS! I'm thrilled to share that our groundbreaking research has been published! 🎉 #Blessed #Research #Innovation"

## Cross-posting from X

When adapting an X post for LinkedIn:
- Expand the hook — you have more space, so add context
- Unroll threads into a single flowing post
- Add a "why this matters for you" angle for the professional audience
- Adjust hashtags for LinkedIn's ecosystem
- Remove X-specific formatting (→ arrows, tweet-style shorthand)
